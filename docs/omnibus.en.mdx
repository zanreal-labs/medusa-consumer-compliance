---
title: Omnibus price history
description: How the plugin records every price a customer saw and computes the lowest price of the 30 days before a reduction, following art. 4 of the Polish price information act and the UOKiK guidance.
---

When a shop announces a price reduction, it must show next to the reduced price
the lowest price of that product in the 30 days before the reduction (art. 4
ust. 2 of the Polish act on price information, implementing art. 6a of Directive
98/6/EC). Computing that number needs a history nobody keeps by default: Medusa
stores the current price, not the prices of last month. This plugin keeps it.

## What is recorded

One row per price a customer could see, per **variant** and **pricing context**,
with `valid_from` and `valid_to`. A context is a currency and, by default, a
region: the plugin asks the pricing module for the calculated price exactly as a
storefront in that region would, so price lists, region rules and their schedules
are all applied. No customer group is passed, on purpose: the history is about
the price offered to the public.

Each row keeps the calculated amount, the regular (original) amount, the price
list id and its type. Rows are never edited; a change closes the open row and
opens a new one. A partial unique index guarantees one open row per series.

Three paths write to it, all through the same idempotent workflow:

| Path | When | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event subscriber | Seconds after a product, variant, price or price list change | The normal case. Events from one save are coalesced into one pass. |
| Hourly sweep | Minute 17 of every hour | A price list that starts or ends on its schedule emits no event. The sweep dates such a change to the list's own `starts_at` or `ends_at`. It is also the backstop for anything an event missed. |
| Backfill | On demand from the admin, and implicitly on the first sweep | Opens a row for every variant after installation. |

Recording the same price twice writes nothing, so all three can overlap safely.
They also run under a lock.

## The reference price

For the current price of a variant, the plugin decides:

1. **Is it an announced reduction?** By default, when the calculated price comes
   from a price list of type `sale` and is lower than the regular price (what the
   stock storefront shows as a struck-through price). With
   `reductionDetection: "lower_than_original"`, any price below the regular price
   counts. Price lists in `ignoredPriceListIds` never count.
2. **When did it start?** The `valid_from` of the current price, extended back over
   earlier rows with the same reduced amount (an unchanged reduction moved to
   another list is still the same reduction).
3. **The window**: the 30 calendar days before that day, in Polish time. Its last
   day is the day before the reduced price could first be bought (UOKiK guidance),
   so a price applied in the morning of the reduction day does not count.
4. **The reference**: the lowest price that applied at any moment in the window,
   earlier promotional prices included (Commission guidance 2021/C 526/02).

The result carries a `reference_label`, which maps to the wording UOKiK expects:

| `reference_label` | Polish label | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `lowest_30_days` | Najniższa cena z 30 dni przed obniżką | Standard case (art. 4 ust. 2). |
| `lowest_since_introduction` | Najniższa cena od wprowadzenia towaru | The product has been on offer for less than 30 days (art. 4 ust. 3). |
| `price_before_reduction` | Cena przed obniżką | Perishable goods (art. 4 ust. 4). |

UOKiK rules out other labels such as "cena referencyjna", "cena omnibus" or
"było". The Store API returns the label text in the requested language as
`reference_label_text`.

### Gradual reductions

Poland did **not** adopt the option of art. 6a(5) of the directive. UOKiK states
that each deeper step of a reduction is measured against the lowest price of the
30 days before that step, which then includes the previous promotional price.
UOKiK's own example: 10 zł from 1 May, 8 zł from 10 June, 4 zł from 15 June. The
15 June reduction is 50%, against 8 zł. That is the default, `per_change`.

`gradualReductions: "keep_first"` keeps the first step's reference for the whole
uninterrupted series, for stores in member states that did adopt the option.

### Products on offer for less than 30 days

The window starts when the product was introduced: the variant's creation date,
or the start of its latest stretch on sale. A product withdrawn for more than 30
days counts as newly introduced on its return; a shorter break does not (UOKiK).
A product introduced and reduced on the same day uses the price it had before the
reduction that day.

### Perishable goods

Products marked `perishable` in the admin use the price from before the **first**
reduction of the current series, and the 30-day rule does not apply (art. 4 ust.
4). UOKiK's working definition is a shelf life of up to 14 days from introduction
to the offer, or special storage conditions. Marking a product is a store
decision.

### Warnings the plugin raises

| Flag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `history_incomplete` | The history starts inside the window and the product existed before. Typical right after installation: the reference covers only what the plugin saw. |
| `reference_not_higher` | The product was this cheap or cheaper within the window. Presenting the price as a reduction would be misleading. |
| `reason: "no_prior_price"` | There is no earlier price at all, for example a product on sale from its first minute. Do not present it as a reduction. |

The percentage `reduction_percent` is always computed against the reference
price, never against the regular price (CJEU C-330/23, UOKiK).

## Discount codes and other reductions outside price lists

UOKiK treats a discount code tied to specific products, or a publicly announced
code for everything, as an announced reduction: the 30-day lowest price must be
shown at the latest where the code is applied, the cart included. Prices obtained
with such codes also become reference prices for later promotions.

The plugin cannot see Medusa promotions on its own, because their effect depends
on a cart. Two tools cover it:

- **`reduction_started_at`** on the variant endpoints evaluates a reduction that
  starts at a given moment, whatever the price list says. Use the promotion's
  start date and show the returned `reference_price` next to the discounted price.
- **External price windows** (admin, price history card, or
  `POST /admin/consumer-compliance/variants/:id/price-history/external`) record a
  price that was obtainable for a period, for example 85 zł with code WEEKEND20.
  It counts towards the lowest price of later reductions but never as the current
  price.

Which promotions are in scope is a policy question; see the open questions in
[Legal basis](./legal.en.mdx).

## Limits worth knowing

- **Tax.** The recorded amount is Medusa's calculated amount. For a currency whose
  prices include tax (PLN in most Polish setups) it is the gross price, which is
  what the law asks for. For tax-exclusive currencies the response says
  `tax_inclusive: false`, and the storefront must apply the same tax it applies to
  the displayed price.
- **Customer groups and personalised prices** are not recorded. A storefront that
  shows a group price as a reduction must handle that case separately.
- **Retention** defaults to 400 days and never goes below the window plus one day.
  Rows still needed by a running reduction are kept regardless.
- **Before installation** nothing was recorded. `history_incomplete` says so,
  per variant, until 30 days have passed.
